# An idea, and a brief

Written 21 September 2026 for an assistant starting cold. You have no prior work to look at,
and that is on purpose: what is wanted is your own thinking, not a variation of somebody
else's.

Joe Scanlon owns the idea and decides. Ask him rather than assuming.

---

## The idea

A video game made for one particular person, with the people they know in it.

You say who the game is for and who is around them. What comes back is a game where that person
is the hero and the people they know turn up in it, with details only that household would
recognise. It plays in a web browser, on anything, with nothing to install.

That is all of it. Who it is for, what it is worth, how it is sold, what it is called and
whether a website is even the right shape for it are all open questions, and they are the
questions you are being asked.

---

## What has to be true whatever you decide

Three things, and none of them are matters of taste.

1. **Children's details.** If a child is anywhere near this, never collect or show photographs,
   voice recordings, surnames, ages, dates of birth, schools, clubs, towns or addresses.
   Anything that stores a child's details needs a GDPR and UK Children's Code review before it
   goes near a real person, and Joe's sign-off.
2. **Nothing that belongs to anyone else.** No real people in the product, no club crests, kits,
   logos or brand marks, no characters from anything.
3. **No invented proof.** No reviews, customer counts, press logos or "most popular" badges
   until they are real. Nothing has sold yet, so there is nothing to claim.

---

## The brief

Come back with options, not a plan. For each of the three questions, give **five different
answers**, then **five more that are wildly different again** from the first five.

**1. How it is presented.** Five ways somebody could meet this thing and understand it in ten
seconds. Then five that break the format: a different medium, a different first contact, a
different thing being sold.

**2. How it is priced.** Five pricing shapes. Then five that change what is being paid for, who
pays, or when. Price is a positioning decision, so for each one say what it makes the thing
*mean* to the person paying.

**3. How it is funded.** Five approaches to Enterprise Ireland, the Local Enterprise Office and
whatever else an Irish company selling into export markets can apply for. Then five that involve
none of those bodies. Amounts and criteria change constantly, so check anything before it goes
in a document and say what you checked and when.

For every option, in two or three lines:

- who it is for, and why they would care
- what it would cost to find out whether it works, in money and in days
- the single thing most likely to kill it
- what evidence you have either way, and where it came from

Then rank them, say which one you would bet on and why, and say plainly which of your own ideas
are weak. Fifteen equally weighted options is homework, not a recommendation.

Where you are guessing, say you are guessing. A confident invention is worse than an honest gap,
because somebody may spend real money on it.

---

## What Joe has already decided

These are answers, not suggestions. Everything not on this list is still open.

- **The ambition is a million dollars of revenue a year, at a high margin, as soon as it can be
  done.** A team of up to about ten people eventually, and being bought would be a good outcome
  rather than a failure. This is a real constraint on your options: anything that cannot grow
  past one person's hands, or that needs craft work on every single order that cannot be
  systematised, is the wrong answer here however charming it is.
- **The money and the time are small.** A few hundred euro and a founder's evenings, for now.
  Something has to take real money in December.
- **The name stays for now.** Nobody has checked whether it can be legally used, so if your best
  idea needs a different one, say so.
- **It does not have to be a website.** A physical product, a service, a licence or something
  sold inside somebody else's shop are all allowed.

## The one question he has deliberately not answered

**Who is it for.** He wants your thinking on this rather than his, so treat it as a fourth
question in the brief: **five different people this could be aimed at**, then **five more from
further out**. For each one, say what the product becomes when it is aimed at them, what they
already spend money on instead, and how you would find fifty of them this month.

Who the buyer is changes everything else in your answer, so do this one first and let the
presentation, the pricing and the funding follow from it.

## Questions still worth putting to Joe

- Who else is involved, and who has to approve what?
- What would make him stop: a number, a date, or a feeling?
