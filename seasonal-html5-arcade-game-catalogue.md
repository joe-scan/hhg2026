# Seasonal HTML5 Arcade Game Catalogue

A catalogue of lightweight browser-game ideas for events, holidays, personal milestones, and promotional campaigns. Every idea is designed around a single immediately understandable action: tap, drag, swipe, aim, move left and right, or match. Build the core once, reskin it with art, sound, copy, and a leaderboard, then host it as a static HTML5 game.

## Product Rules

- A player should understand the objective in five seconds.
- Controls should work with mouse, touch, and keyboard where practical.
- Aim for 30–90 second runs, with a restart button available instantly.
- Use a score, streak, timer, or personal-best target to create replay value.
- Keep the game bundle static: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images/audio, and optional local leaderboard storage.
- Build reusable mechanic families rather than fifty unrelated games: runner, catcher, stacker, thrower, tapper, trace game, memory game, sorter, match-3, and bubble shooter.

## Birthday

Birthdays are evergreen, personal, and easy to customise with a name, age, colours, photo, or party theme. These work for invitation pages, venue screens, brand activations, and post-party links.

### Cake Stack

Cake layers fall from above and the player taps or clicks to drop each layer onto the cake below. Layers that land off-centre make the tower wobble, so the run ends when the cake collapses; change frosting colours, candles, and decorations for each birthday.

### Present Catch

The player moves a party bag or basket left and right to catch falling presents. Gifts add points, while empty boxes, party mess, or prank gifts cost a life; increasing drop speed makes a one-minute mode feel tense.

### Balloon Pop Aim

Balloons rise across the screen and the player drags back then releases a dart, pin, or party-popper shot. Score for colour targets and consecutive hits, with a rare golden balloon acting as a high-value bonus.

### Candle Blow Timing

Candles flicker around a cake and light up in a short sequence. The player taps each flame at the right moment to blow it out; successful chains create a birthday-wish combo, while mistimed taps relight a candle.

### Party Hat Toss

The player aims and tosses party hats onto moving party guests. Hats that land squarely earn a perfect score, and the guests can move faster or change direction each round without adding complicated controls.

## Anniversaries and Weddings

These games should feel warm and celebratory rather than competitive or punishing. Personalisation is the hook: names, date, venue colours, a couple’s photos, and messages from guests.

### Ring Toss

The player drags to aim and releases rings at pegs, bottles, or wedding-themed targets. Distance, moving targets, and a small wind indicator make the mechanic deeper while remaining clear on a phone screen.

### Flower Bouquet Builder

Flowers arrive on a conveyor belt and the player drags the requested blooms into a bouquet before time expires. Each round can show a simple recipe card, such as three roses and two white lilies, making it easy to reskin by season.

### Photo Frame Match

A classic card-memory game uses photos, illustrated memories, venue icons, or romantic symbols. The player flips two cards at a time and tries to clear the board in the fewest turns or fastest time.

### Champagne Pop

A bottle’s pressure meter moves back and forth, and the player taps in the highlighted zone to launch the cork cleanly. Perfect timing starts a celebratory spray and builds a combo; poor timing gives a smaller pop but keeps the game friendly.

### Heart Path Runner

A couple, ring, or heart runs along a winding path while the player jumps thorns, puddles, and obstacles. Roses, notes, and stars serve as collectibles, and the game can use a single tap for jumping.

## Graduation

Graduation games suit schools, colleges, recruitment campaigns, and family celebrations. They can carry a year, school colours, course names, or a message at the end of each round.

### Cap Toss

The player taps or drags to throw a graduation cap toward a moving target. Landing on a graduate’s head, a hoop, or a celebratory banner gives points; later rounds add gentle wind and smaller targets.

### Diploma Dash

A graduate runs through a campus scene, jumping puddles and obstacles while collecting diplomas and stars. It is a standard endless runner with graduation art, so it is quick to build and easy to reuse.

### Yearbook Memory

Players reveal cards and match graduation icons such as caps, books, diplomas, and school badges. Use a short countdown and a best-time score to keep the simple memory mechanic replayable.

### Confetti Catch

The player moves a graduation cap or open hands to catch falling confetti. Gold stars and school-colour confetti score points, while unwanted items such as crumpled paper reduce a life or break a streak.

### Podium Stack

Blocks descend and must be placed in a stable tower to build a graduation podium. Accurate placement earns a neat stack and a combo multiplier; uneven placement makes the tower lean.

## Baby Shower and New Baby

Keep the art gentle, inclusive, and adaptable to the family’s preferred style. The best versions are short, low-pressure mini-games that work well as party links or QR-code activities.

### Bottle Catch

The player moves a basket, stroller, or pair of hands to catch falling bottles. Bottles add points, while spills, dropped toys, or non-baby items reduce the score and make the screen busier.

### Onesie Sort

Onesies travel along a conveyor belt and must be dragged to the matching size, colour, or hanger. The mechanic is a straightforward sorter, which makes it ideal for branded baby products as well.

### Rattle Shake Timing

A moving beat marker crosses a timing bar and the player taps as it enters the target zone. Perfect hits make the rattle sparkle and build a streak; the same engine can become a rhythm game for any event.

### Stroller Push Runner

The player guides a stroller through a park with one-button jumps over cracks, puddles, and toys. Collect bottles, stars, or letters while the background changes to give the run a sense of progress.

### Baby Name Match

Players flip cards and match baby-name cards with cute supporting icons or matching letter cards. It works particularly well when the family provides a shortlist of names or party guests submit their guesses.

## New Year’s Eve

New Year games need immediate spectacle, short sessions, and a score worth sharing. Fireworks, countdown clocks, confetti, and sparkles make the same basic mechanics feel event-specific.

### Firework Aim

The player drags back and releases rockets toward glowing sky targets. A direct hit produces a larger firework and a score multiplier, while the target positions become less predictable as the round continues.

### Midnight Countdown Runner

The player races toward a giant midnight clock, jumping obstacles and collecting time orbs. The environment can shift from 11:59 to a full fireworks scene as the score rises.

### Champagne Pop

The player taps at the ideal point on a moving timing meter to pop bottles. It is a quick reaction game with a satisfying animation, so it works in a single 20-second round or a high-score format.

### Resolution Sorter

New Year resolutions move down a conveyor belt and the player drags them into the right category, such as health, money, travel, or learning. Incorrect sorting ends a streak, while fast correct decisions drive the score.

### Sparkler Trace

A glowing line appears briefly and the player traces it with a finger or mouse before the spark burns out. Start with stars and numbers, then increase difficulty with longer patterns and a tighter timer.

## Lunar New Year

Lunar New Year supports bright visual design, red-and-gold palettes, lanterns, zodiac art, and coin-collection loops. Avoid collapsing the holiday into a generic aesthetic; use respectful, specific visual references and localised copy.

### Lantern Pop

Lanterns rise from the bottom of the screen and the player taps them before they drift away. Different lantern colours can mean points, time bonuses, or hazards, and the growing pace makes it instantly arcade-like.

### Dragon Runner

The player guides a dragon through clouds, collecting coins and avoiding obstacles. A single tap can control a jump or flap, making it a fast reskin of an endless runner or flappy-style game.

### Red Envelope Catch

The player shifts a basket or character left and right to catch falling red envelopes. Valuable envelopes create short bonus rounds, while distracting objects encourage quick decisions rather than complex play.

### Zodiac Match-3

Players swap neighbouring zodiac-animal tiles to create rows of three or more. Give each level a target number of a specific animal or a limited number of moves, then rotate artwork each year.

### Dumpling Stack

Dumplings drop one at a time and the player positions each carefully to form a tall, stable stack. The physics can be forgiving at first and gradually become more unstable as the stack grows.

## Diwali

Diwali games can focus on lights, colour, sweets, rangoli, and festive celebration. Avoid fireworks-only framing; lighting diyas and making patterns give the theme more variety.

### Diya Light-Up

A sequence of diyas lights briefly, then the player repeats the sequence by tapping them in order. This is a Simon-style memory game with a clear visual payoff as the whole display glows.

### Rangoli Trace

The player traces a simple rangoli path before a timer runs down. Clean tracing raises a quality meter, and finished patterns unlock new colour palettes or more intricate designs.

### Firework Aim

The player launches fireworks into designated sky zones using drag-and-release aiming. It shares a reusable projectile mechanic with New Year campaigns but has Diwali-specific art, sound, and reward screens.

### Sweet Box Sort

Different sweets travel down a conveyor and must be dragged into matching gift boxes. Orders can be shown as a picture card, so players need no language-heavy instructions.

### Coin Catch

The player catches falling coins in a bowl or tray while avoiding broken pots and clutter. It is a simple left-right catcher that can support score multipliers, rare coins, and short bonus rounds.

## Eid

Eid games should be built with care and a flexible approach to regional traditions and languages. Lanterns, crescent moons, sharing, gifts, and celebratory food can all support light, family-friendly mechanics.

### Moon Sighter

Clouds move across a night sky and the player taps when a crescent moon is visible. The game rewards reaction time and accuracy, with false taps costing a short delay or ending a streak.

### Lantern Hang

The player drags swinging lanterns onto hooks at the right moment. Correct placement creates a glowing display, while swinging motion adds challenge without needing a complicated rule set.

### Date Catch

A basket slides left and right to catch falling dates. Fresh dates add points, while spoiled or falling non-food objects break a combo; the rate increases naturally across a short run.

### Prayer Rug Memory

Players turn over cards to match respectful decorative rug patterns. This is a quiet, accessible option for mobile, with difficulty controlled through card count and time rather than fast reflexes.

### Eidi Envelope Toss

The player aims envelopes at moving hands, boxes, or targets. Distance-based scoring and a small number of throws give it an easy score-attack format.

## Hanukkah

Hanukkah naturally fits light-up, spinning, stacking, and catching games. Keep symbols accurate and make the visuals warm rather than relying on generic winter imagery.

### Menorah Light

The game shows a growing sequence of candles and the player taps the matching candle order. Each successful round lights more of the menorah, giving visible progression as difficulty rises.

### Gelt Catch

The player moves a tray or bag to catch falling chocolate coins. Bonus coins can trigger a short magnet effect, while non-coin objects create a simple risk-reward choice.

### Dreidel Spin Timing

A spinning dreidel slows down and the player taps to try to stop it on a target symbol. Runs can be based on accuracy, streaks, or a small set of attempts.

### Latke Stack

Players align falling latkes into a stack without letting it topple. It is a one-button balance game that gets harder through narrower pieces and faster drops.

### Oil Jug Pour

The player holds to pour oil and releases at the correct fill line. Overfilling spills and costs a life, while accurate pours create a clean, satisfying visual result.

### Candle Light Sequence

### Papel Picado Trace

The player traces cut-paper-inspired decorative paths with a finger or mouse. Accuracy and speed determine the final score, while the finished banner animates overhead.

### Pot of Gold Catch

### Clover Match-3

### Shamrock Hunt

### Rainbow Runner

## Gaming Holidays and Retro Themes

Gaming-focused dates and retro events are a natural audience fit, but trademarks and specific game art still require permission. Use original pixels, generic controllers, and fictional cartridges when licensing is not in place.

Players swap pixel-art tiles to make rows of three or more. Retro effects, chiptune-style original audio, and simple icons make it feel nostalgic without copying a particular game.

### Controller Button Tap

### Cartridge Stack

## Earth Day, Peace Day, and Global Causes

Cause-based games should pair entertainment with a clear, accurate call to action on the end screen. Keep the cause message separate from the scoring loop so the game remains enjoyable and the message remains understandable.

### Tree Plant Runner

The player runs through a damaged landscape and plants trees by collecting seeds or hitting planting points. As the score rises, the scenery becomes greener, giving immediate visual feedback.

### Recycle Sort

Waste items move down a conveyor and players drag each into the correct recycling, compost, or rubbish bin. This is one of the strongest educational arcade formats because the action mirrors the real-world behaviour.

### Globe Trace

Players trace simple continent outlines or environmental symbols before time expires. The game should use accurate, simplified shapes and avoid presenting it as a geography test unless that is the stated purpose.

### Dove Release Aim

The player aims to release doves or peaceful symbols through sky targets. Keep the objective celebratory and abstract, with scoring based on clean target hits.

### Peace Symbol Match

Players flip cards to match peace-themed icons, flags only where appropriate, and original illustrations. A calm, untimed mode can make this suitable for classrooms and younger audiences.

## Minimal Technical Stack

Use plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with Canvas for the game scene, or use Phaser if you want a mature HTML5 game framework. Keep assets in a single static build and deploy to any static host; no backend is required unless you add accounts, prizes, or a global leaderboard.

For each game, include a title screen, one-line instructions, play button, pause/mute controls, game-over score screen, replay button, and a shareable result image or link. Add responsive scaling, touch input, keyboard fallback, accessible contrast, and reduced-motion support from the start.

## Asset Checklist Per Reskin

- Background, foreground, player, collectible, hazard, target, and one or two power-up sprites
- Six to ten sound effects and one short original music loop
- Title card, instruction panel, score panel, game-over panel, and optional sponsor/end card
- Three difficulty settings driven by speed, spawn rate, target size, and timer length
- Event-specific copy, including localised language where needed
- A rights check for brands, logos, character likenesses, music, and culturally specific imagery
