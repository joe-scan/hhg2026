# Happy Hero Games: business plan

Written 19 September 2026, rewritten 20 September 2026 for a US-first launch in US dollars. Every number here is an assumption to test, not a forecast. The first version of this plan (two siblings, sold as a rivalry game) is in the Fionn vs Sean repo; this version makes one person the hero. That person is usually a child, which is who the numbers below assume, but the product and the copy work for a grown-up too.

The doc keeps British spelling. The site uses US spelling, because the buyers are American.

## 1. In one paragraph

People buy a personalised arcade game for someone special, usually a child, for a birthday or Christmas. That person is the hero and their own family are the opponents, with the dog as referee, and it ends with "HAPPY BIRTHDAY, AOIFE!" in lights. It plays on any phone. A free demo on the landing page builds the hero in pixels in under a minute; the game is $79, made in 48 hours and checked by a person. Every game comes with a free first month of the club, which is $8 a month or $69 a year after that and keeps sending new duels. There is also a $149 gift box with the family's recorded voices in it.

## 2. The honest summary

- **The demand is proven.** [Muksun Games](https://muksungames.com/) sells custom games starring your family for $99 (Android and Windows only). [Wonderbly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderbly) sold millions of personalised children's books and was bought by Penguin Random House in 2025. We need to be better, not first.
- **The US is the market.** Roughly 73 million under-18s, a culture of spending on birthday presents, and ad platforms that will sell us as much reach as we can pay for. Ireland is where Joe is and where the first hand-built games get made; it is not where we launch.
- **One hero is a bigger market than two rivals.** Every child has a birthday. Birthdays happen all year, so this is less seasonal than a Christmas-only gift, and it suits the grandparent buying for one grandchild.
- **"$50K MRR quickly" is still the wrong target.** Games are one-off purchases. On the model in section 8, **total monthly revenue first passes $50K in October 2027** and stays above it from then on, including the January after Christmas. **Recurring revenue alone is much further out.** At $69 a year, after fees and an allowance for refunds and failed renewals, a club member is worth about $5.20 a month, so $50K of club MRR needs about **9,500 paying members**. The model reaches 3,160 by September 2028. On these rates 9,500 is 2030, or 2029 if monthly cancellations drop from 7% to about 4%. The club is an add-on to a game, never a way of buying one, so it can only grow as fast as game sales.
- **The make-or-break number is human time per game.** At $79, an order earns about $23 after advertising if a person can check it in 30 minutes. At 90 minutes it loses money.
- **The free demo is the sales engine.** Someone types a name, sees the hero in pixels, and plays four duels against their own family. That is the moment that sells it, the same way Wonderbly's preview sold books.

## 3. Customers

In order of how much we spend to reach them.

| Who | Why they buy | Where to reach them |
|---|---|---|
| US parents of 5 to 12-year-olds | A birthday present nobody else will give, that the child plays with the family | Instagram and TikTok ads, Facebook parent groups, class group chats |
| US grandparents, godparents, aunts and uncles | Something personal that isn't another toy, and easy to give from far away | Gift cards, Facebook, search in November and December |
| UK, Irish, Canadian and Australian parents | Same reason, same language, no translation work. Second wave, once the US ads are paying back. | The same ads, run later, at lower budgets |
| Partners and grown-up children buying for an adult | A Father's Day, Mother's Day or milestone-birthday present with the whole family in it. Untested; the model below counts none of it. | Search and gift guides around those days |
| Families with two kids close in age | The sibling second hero turns it into a rivalry game (the Fionn vs Sean original) | Add-on at checkout |

Why the US first and not Ireland. Ireland has about 60,000 births a year against roughly 3.6 million in the US, and Irish parents will not carry a paid-ads business. The old plan's argument was that a small, connected market gives fast word of mouth, and that is still true, which is why the first hand-built games come from Joe's own network in Ireland. But the first families are research, not a launch market. The money goes into US ads from the start.

What the US costs us: a merchant of record for sales tax in about 45 states, US spelling and US date formats, support hours that overlap the east coast, and no physical box until there is a US fulfilment partner (section 11).

## 5. Pricing

| Product | Price | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| The demo | Free | 4 duels and a boss, built in the browser |
| The game | $79 one time | 10 duels, 2 bosses, up to 6 family members, custom lines. Theirs forever. The main product. |
| The club | $8 a month or $69 a year, on top of a game | First month free with every game. A new duel every month, seasonal games at Halloween and Christmas, add family members any time, scores that carry over. |
| The gift box | $149 | The game, recorded family voices, a printed box and card, tracked delivery. No subscription, because you cannot gift a direct debit. |
| Sibling second hero | +$25 | A brother or sister as a second hero: turns it into a two-player rivalry |
| Gift cards | $79, $149, or $69 for 12 months of the club prepaid | The giver pays, the parent fills in the questionnaire. The prepaid club year goes to a family that already has a game, or alongside one. |

**The club is an add-on, never a bundle.** Everyone buys the game at $79 first. The club then sits on top of a game the family already owns and adds a new duel every month. It is not another way to get the game, and it is not a cheaper door into the product: there is no path to a personalised game that costs less than $79. Joe decided this on 20 September 2026, and it closes the hole an earlier draft of this plan had, where $8 would have bought the $79 product.

**The first month of the club comes free with every game.** That makes it an upsell rather than a barrier: the family gets their game, plays it, and a month later a new duel turns up. They decide then whether to keep paying. Nobody is asked to commit to a subscription at the moment they are buying a present.

**The rule that governs all of it: a gift never expires, and the subscription only ever adds.** Cancel the club whenever you like and everything the family already has keeps working, at the same link, forever, including every duel the club has already delivered. That is not a policy we have to enforce; it falls out of the architecture, because a game is static files sitting in a folder. Nothing they have been given is ever taken back. This is the promise that makes a subscription acceptable on a children's present, and it should never be softened to protect a churn number.

**Cost of one $79 game.** Merchant-of-record and card fees about 5% plus $0.50, so $4.45 on $79 and $4.70 on an $84 order with a sibling. Human check 30 minutes at $28 an hour, $14. AI drafting $2 to $5. Hosting under $0.50. That is about **$61 left on an average order of $84** before advertising, and about **$23 after** a blended $38 to win the customer. At a $45 acquisition cost it is $16. At 90 minutes of checking instead of 30 it is under zero.

**Cost of one $149 gift box.** The costs above plus a printed box and card about $12, voice editing about 15 minutes ($7), and tracked delivery inside the UK, Ireland and the EU $12 to $18. About **$89 before advertising**. It earns more than the game but not twice as much, so price it for the gift, not the margin.

**Cost of one club member.** Almost nothing per member per month, because the monthly duel is built once and shipped to everybody. The cost is the duel itself: one new duel a month, every month, forever, whatever the member count. See the treadmill risk in section 11. The free first month costs nothing extra either, since that duel is already being made for the paying members.

**The gift box and the club do not mix, and that is deliberate.** You cannot give someone a direct debit, so the $149 box is a finished present with no subscription attached. It still carries the free club month, like every game, and whoever receives it can start paying if they want more.

Prices on the site and prices in this plan have to be the same figures. If one moves, the other moves in the same commit.

## 6. Growth

1. **The demo is the ad.** Every ad and post links straight to the builder, not a sales page. Measure: builder starts, demo plays, then purchases.
2. **US paid social first.** Instagram and TikTok, creative that opens on a child seeing their own name in pixels. The whole business depends on whether blended acquisition lands nearer $30 than $45.
3. **Reaction videos.** A child seeing themselves in lights for the first time is the best advert we can buy. Pay US families, with consent, to film it, and run those.
4. **Kids share it.** Share on the finale sends a picture plus the game's link. Every paid game carries a "make one for your family" link with a referral code: $10 off for the friend, a bonus duel or a free club month for the family. Target 15% of orders from referrals by month 6.
5. **Gift cards** for grandparents and faraway relatives, pushed hardest in November and December. The $69 prepaid club gift is the one to push, because it hands us a member who has already paid for a year.
6. **Then the rest of the English-speaking market:** the UK and Ireland together from the third quarter of 2027, Canada and Australia in 2028. Same copy, same ads, a currency switch and a different tax registration. Australia is worth doing for the reversed seasons alone: their summer birthdays land in our quiet months.
7. **Later:** a club or school version, the whole team as heroes, sold as a fundraiser. A separate bet, parked until the family product works.

## 7. Operations

The paid game follows `docs/product.md`: questionnaire, generator, a person checks it, delivery in 48 hours or on the chosen date. The demo already proves the generator's first half, config to sprites and duels. What's missing: the questionnaire, payments, order storage with consent and deletion, the AI drafting step, a checking tool, and the rest of the duel library.

**Payments go through a merchant of record, Paddle or Lemon Squeezy.** They take about 5% plus $0.50, which is more than Stripe, and in exchange they become the seller of record and handle US state sales tax, EU VAT and UK VAT, including registration, filing and the EU's one-stop shop. For a one-person business selling digital goods into 45 US states from Ireland, that is the reason to use them. Revisit when revenue is large enough that the difference between 5% and 3% pays for an accountant.

Support runs from Ireland, which is five hours ahead of New York. Answer US email by early afternoon Irish time so a parent who writes in the evening has a reply when they wake up.

The physical box ships from Ireland and is sold to the UK, Ireland and the EU only. See section 11.

## 8. The revenue model

Assumptions, all to test:

- Hand-built orders from October 2026, mostly to Joe's own network in Ireland, then a generator from spring 2027.
- Price rises from $49 for the first families to $79 by January 2027. 20% of orders add a sibling at $25, so the average paid order is about $84 once gift boxes and siblings are mixed in.
- Every club member has bought a game first, so club members are a subset of buyers and never an extra customer.
- The club opens April 2027. 60% of buyers take the free first month, and 60% of those go on paying, so 36% of buyers become paying members, one month after their game. 20% of earlier buyers take the free month at launch on the same conversion. After that, 7% cancel each month. The mix of $8 monthly and $69 annual works out at about $6.00 a member a month in billings and about $5.20 after fees, refunds and failed renewals.
- The club members column counts paying members only. A family on their free month is worth $0, which is why the column starts a month behind the games.
- Blended acquisition cost $38 per new game, inside an assumed range of $30 to $45 in the US. This is the single number most likely to be wrong, and the one to measure first.
- Christmas brings about 2.5 times a normal month, less than a pure gift product because birthdays fill the year.

What it says:

- **Total monthly revenue first passes $50K in October 2027** and does not drop below it again. The month that proves the point is January 2028 at $59.5K: a Christmas-only product would have collapsed there, and a birthday product does not.
- **Club MRR is a different and much slower story.** At about $5.20 net a member a month, $50K of recurring revenue needs roughly **9,500 paying members**. The model has 3,160 by September 2028, worth about $16.4K net. Worse, at 7% monthly cancellations the club settles wherever churn balances joins: 0.07 times 9,500 is 665 new paying members a month, and at a 36% conversion that means about **1,850 new games every month, sustained**. **On these rates that is 2030.** Cut cancellations to 4% and the same 9,500 holds on about 1,050 new games a month, which the model reaches in 2029. Churn matters more than growth here, and it is the number to attack. Because every member bought a game first, the club can never grow faster than game sales do.
- **December 2027 means 1,500 orders**, which is 750 hours of checking at 30 minutes each, about $21K of wages and roughly five people working full time for the month. Hire and train seasonal checkers from October, or close Christmas orders early.
- **Ad spend is the whole risk.** December 2027 spends $57K to earn $132.3K. If blended acquisition comes in at $45 rather than $38, that month's ad bill is $67.5K and the margin after everything is thin. If it comes in at $30 the business is comfortable. Nothing else in the model moves the answer as much.

## 10. What would kill it

Decide these before starting, and change course if they happen:

- Blended acquisition cost above $60 after $10K of US ad spend, with no creative that beats it.
- Fewer than 5% of demo players join the launch list, or fewer than 50 paid orders from the first 1,000 demo plays.
- Human time per game still over 60 minutes after 200 orders.
- Under 30% of families pressing Share.
- Fewer than 20% of buyers still paying for the club three months after the free month: then drop the MRR goal and run it as a gift business, which can still be a good one.

## 11. Risks

- **Children's data.** One leak would end it. Collect little, show less, private by default, reviewed by a lawyer before launch. US children's privacy law (COPPA) sits on top of GDPR and the UK Children's Code, and the FTC has been fining on it. Get US advice, not just Irish advice, before the first US order.
- **The club treadmill.** Every paying member is owed a new duel every month, forever, and so is everyone on a free first month. That obligation never goes away and never gets cheaper per member; it just gets harder to skip as the member count grows, because a missed month is a refund request from everybody at once. Build a duel backlog of at least six months before opening the club, treat the monthly drop as a fixed cost of about a week of work, and design the seasonal games to be reusable year on year. If the backlog runs dry, pause new sign-ups rather than ship a thin month. Note that the free first month puts every buyer on the treadmill from day one, whether they ever pay or not.
- **The physical box does not travel.** Tracked shipping from Ireland to the US is $20 to $30 and takes a week or more, and the buyer can be hit with a customs charge on delivery, which is the worst possible thing to happen to a present. So the box is sold to the UK, Ireland and the EU only. The US gets the game, the voices and the club, and gets the box when there is a US print and fulfilment partner, which is a 2028 job.
- **Cannibalising the game.** The club is an add-on and there is no way to get a game for less than $79, so the old hole is closed. The thing to watch now is the opposite one: if the free first month trains people to expect new duels for nothing, conversion off the free month is what suffers, and the whole club number in section 8 moves with it. Measure it from the first cohort.
- **Currency and the Irish base.** Revenue is in dollars, costs are largely in euro and sterling. A 10% move in the rate moves the margin on a $79 game by about $6. Not fatal, worth watching.
- **The name.** "Hero Games" is used by a Beijing publisher and a US tabletop publisher; there's a mobile game called Happy Hero. Search USPTO before spending on the brand in the US, and EUIPO before spending in Europe.
- **Quality slipping.** It only works while the jokes feel made for that family. Don't cut the human check to save money.
- **Copycats.** Anyone with AI tools can make one game. The edge is the demo, the speed, the family depth, the privacy and the share loop.
- **Seasonality and cash.** Christmas still spikes. Keep enough cash to hire checkers ahead of it and to carry a $57K ad month.
