# Who else sells this

Written 20 September 2026, from pages captured the same day. The raw captures are in `docs/research/competitor-pages-raw.md` so any claim here can be checked against what the sites actually said. Prices are theirs, as advertised, in their own currency.

## The shape of the market

Four bands, and they barely compete with each other:

| Band | Price | Who | What you actually get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty | $30 to £5 | Ready Player U, Minigamegift, the Etsy long tail | A template with your names and a few answers dropped in. Instant or near it. |
| Made for you | $99 to $179 | **Muksun, Gift Games, us** | A real game with your people in it, built by someone in a few days. |
| Commissioned | $250 to $450 | Bday Game | One person builds you something from scratch over weeks. A booking, not a product. |
| Bespoke luxury | up to $67,000 | Amuze Me (since 2011) | A studio makes a real game as a portrait. Historic, not a competitor. |

The category is older than it looks. Wikipedia dates it to the start of the industry as a luxury gift, with Kevin Smith commissioning a custom game for Ben Affleck and Jennifer Lopez in 2002, and TinglyGames automating it as "greeting games" in 2014. Nobody has won it.

## The ones that matter

### Muksun Games (muksungames.com)
The closest competitor. $99 Classic: three custom levels, 20 to 40 minutes, up to five heroes, ready in four to seven days. $399 from scratch: five levels, up to seven heroes, seven to ten days. Rush delivery is **+$49 for three days**. Photos become the in-game hero. 4.77 from 61 reviews. One free round of tweaks. Runs on Shopify.

- **Their strongest move:** "Pay now, tell us about your heroes after, takes about five minutes." They take the money before the work of filling in a form. Also a free printable gift certificate the moment you order, so there is something to hand over on the day.
- **Their weakness, and it is enormous:** Android and Windows only. No iPhone, no iPad, no Mac. Half the gift-buying world cannot play the thing they just bought. They also lead with $99 for 20 to 40 minutes of play.
- **Take from them:** rush delivery as a paid upgrade, the instant printable certificate, and the deadline-aware checkout that says "order today, delivered by Sunday 27th" and offers rush when the date is too close.

### Ready Player U (Shopify, US)
The volume player. "Legends of Love Custom Game" at **$30.89**, with **1,873 reviews at 4.8**. Quiz-shaped: you write three questions with the right answer first, upload photos which get pixelated in black and white, and the game is a browser link. The money is in the print upsells: unframed print, framed print, canvas, each showing the game art with a QR code to play it. Seasonal products, like "Legendary Dad" for Father's Day.

- **What it proves:** the print upsell is real, and a QR code that plays the game is a product people frame. That is the poster we already built, validated by someone else's 1,873 reviews.
- **What to watch:** there is a one-star review calling it a scam, with a public reply saying the link was already sent. At their volume, delivery confusion is the failure mode.
- **The uncomfortable question:** they sell at a third of our price and have thirty times our proof. We are not competing on price, so our answer has to be that a $99 game is a different thing: five games, eight people, checked by a person. If ten parents tell us it isn't, the price is wrong.

### Gift Games (giftgames.store, Poland, trading since 2020)
Pixel-art zombie adventure where the hero is the recipient. **€37.50 digital, €45 DIY, €60 boxed**, free EU delivery, four working days, and English, Polish and Spanish. Fifty personalisation elements, nine cover designs, an hour of play. The box edition ships the game on a **flash drive in a cassette shell**, with a printed cover made from the recipient's photos and your message on the back.

- **Take from them:** the physical object is a cover and a medium, not a parcel of stuff. Also the honesty of "1h unforgettable gameplay" stated up front, and three languages for a three-person operation.
- **Their weakness:** one story, zombies, aimed at gamers and couples. Not a children's product, and the pixel art is generic.

### Minigamegift (Minigamegift LTD, UK)
Instant and cheap. Pick a template (Love Notes, Ghost Whisperer, Rescue!), write messages and questions, design characters, pay, and the play link lands in your inbox in moments. Browser-based, Stripe, 20% off for joining the newsletter. No changes after purchase, stated plainly.

- **Take from them:** the six-step "how it works" written as steps a nervous buyer can follow, and instant delivery as the entire pitch.
- **Their weakness:** it is a greeting card. Nothing is made for you, so there is no reason to pay more than a tenner.

### Bday Game (bdaygame.com, one developer, Peter)
The opposite end. **$250 narrative, $450+ fully custom**, weeks of work, a Skype call to shape the story together, delivered by WeTransfer, Windows only. You have to email and ask whether he has capacity.

- **What it proves:** people pay $450 for this when the story is theirs. His references are a marriage proposal and a man opening his own pharmacy, not "beat the zombies".
- **Why he cannot scale:** he is the product. Capacity is a calendar, and the site says so.

### Also in the file, not competitors
- **Play Curious**: a studio making serious games for companies and NGOs. Different business entirely.
- **GameGift** (game-gift.netlify.app): a £5 side project posted to dev.to, three templates, and the note in the capture says it looks closed. The gravestone is instructive: templates plus a fiver is not a business.

## Who we are actually competing with for the money

A parent with $99 and a birthday in two weeks is not comparing personalised games. They are choosing between this and every other made-for-them gift:

- **Songfinch**, a custom song written and recorded by a real musician, $199 and up, five to ten days. Revenue went $150,000 in 2019, $1.45M in 2020, past $5M in 2021, and they have since delivered over 400,000 songs and paid out $40M to artists. They pay the artist about 55% of the order. **This is the closest thing to a proof that our number is reachable**, and the model is worth copying: a network of human makers, paid per piece, with the company owning the form, the marketing and the quality bar.
- **Wonderbly**, personalised children's books, millions sold, bought by Penguin Random House in 2025.
- **Etsy**, where "custom video game gift" is a search term with its own landing page. That is a discovery channel we are not in, and it is where a lot of this money is spent.
- **AI game generators**, which will keep pushing the novelty band towards free. They cannot write a joke only one family understands, and they cannot check it.

## What the landscape tells us, in order of what I would act on

1. **Nobody sells a personalised demo.** Ready Player U has a demo, but it is their generic game. Muksun and Gift Games show screenshots and a form. We let someone type a name and play their own hero in under a minute, before paying. That is our single biggest advantage and the whole page should be built around it. It already is.
2. **Muksun cannot run on an iPhone.** Every piece of our marketing should say "works on any phone, nothing to install", because their $99 customer finds that out after paying.
3. **Rush delivery is proven money.** +$49 for three days, offered exactly when the buyer types a date that is too close. We deliver in 48 hours as standard, so ours is "tomorrow, +$39", and it sells itself on panic.
4. **Something to unwrap, instantly.** Muksun's free printable certificate and Gift Games' DIY edition both solve the same problem: digital gifts are terrible to hand over. We should give every order a printable card the moment they pay, free.
5. **Prints are the upsell, not the product.** Ready Player U proves people buy a framed version of the art with a QR code on it. Our $179 poster is the same bet at a higher price, and it should be sold as a framed option too.
6. **Reviews are the currency and we have none.** They have 1,873 and 61. Collecting the first fifty, with the reaction videos, matters more than any feature on the roadmap.
7. **Take the money before the form.** Muksun's "pay now, tell us about your heroes after" removes the biggest drop-off in the funnel. Our questionnaire should come after payment, not before.
8. **Nobody owns children and families.** Gift Games is gamers and couples, Ready Player U is couples and dads, Minigamegift is romance, Bday Game is whoever emails him. Muksun is the only one aimed at the same place as us, and they are aimed at everyone. "Their birthday, their family, their jokes" is a gap.
9. **Languages are cheap reach, and this one is done.** A Polish two-person shop sells in three languages. Done on 20 September 2026: Spanish, German, French, Italian and Irish, pages and game alike, built from one file of words per language.
10. **Be explicit about delivery.** The one bad review in the whole capture is a delivery misunderstanding. A clear "here is your link, here is where it lives, here is what to do if you cannot see it" email prevents the only kind of one-star we are likely to get.

## Where we win, and where we do not

| | Us | Best of them |
|---|---|---|
| Try before buying | Your own child, playing, in a minute | A generic demo, or screenshots |
| Devices | Any browser | Android and Windows (Muksun) |
| Who is in it | Up to eight family and friends, plus the pet | Up to five (Muksun), two (most) |
| Speed | 48 hours | Instant (templates), 3 days rush (Muksun) |
| Made by a person | Every game read and played before it ships | Muksun and Bday Game yes, the rest no |
| Price | $99 | $30.89 to $99 to $450 |
| Proof | None yet | 1,873 reviews (Ready Player U) |
| Photos | We refuse them | Everyone else uses them |

That last line is the one to think hardest about. Every competitor turns a photograph into the hero, and "that's literally my face" is a strong moment. We refuse photos on purpose, because holding pictures of children is a risk we do not want and a promise we can sell. It is the right call for a children's product, but it costs us a wow that our competitors get for free, so the pixel hero has to be good enough to stand in for it. That is an argument for making the builder better, not for changing the rule.
